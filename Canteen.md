ROLE
Act like a senior food-service consultant who's actually turned around college 
canteens before — someone who backs every call with real numbers, not vague advice.

THE SITUATION
I've got ₹10,000, one-time, to fix up my college canteen for a week — Monday to 
Sunday, 8am to 6pm. That ₹10,000 covers everything: ingredients, packaging, any 
signage or promo, plus a safety buffer. No top-ups mid-week. Stove, fridge, 
utensils, and seating already exist, so don't budget for those.

Assume it's a normal Indian college: roughly 1800–2200 students, 18–23 years old, 
spending ₹40–80 a day on food. Mix of hostellers (need all meals from the canteen) 
and day-scholars (just snacks/lunch), plus 1–2 rival food stalls nearby competing 
for the same crowd. If you need to assume anything beyond this, state it upfront 
and keep going — don't stop to ask me questions.

THE GOAL
More students eating there, happier students, no running out of money, minimal 
food waste, and no chaotic queues at lunch.

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
1. A menu — 8 to 15 real Indian canteen items with cost/unit, price, and margin 
   for each. At least 2 cheap high-margin items to pull people in, and one 
   "flagship" item the rival stalls don't have.
2. The actual pricing logic — cost-plus math, how it stacks against rival prices, 
   any ₹X vs ₹(X−1) psychology, and combo pricing worked out if you use it.
3. Exactly how the ₹10,000 splits — ingredients, packaging, marketing, contingency 
   — and it has to add up to ₹10,000 or less.
4. How much to prepare each day (Mon–Sun) per item, with your reasoning — 
   weekday vs weekend, lunch rush, any exam-week bump. How batches shrink on 
   perishables to cut waste, and how Day 1–2's actual sales should adjust what 
   gets made for the rest of the week.
5. How you'd manage the crowd — a token or staggered-order system, a pre-order 
   option for regular hostellers, a discount window for slow hours, one daily 
   limited "special" for urgency without overproducing, and a free way to track 
   daily demand (like a tally sheet).
6. A day-by-day calendar — what's special that day, expected footfall, what 
   you're pushing, one operational note.
7. The week's P&L — revenue (footfall × conversion × price, shown), total cost, 
   gross profit, and how many transactions I need to break even.
8. Top 3 things that could go wrong (an item flopping, a price war, ingredient 
   costs spiking) and your specific in-budget fix for each.
9. At least 4 marketing ideas costing ₹0–300 each (WhatsApp groups, a poster, 
   first-20-customers freebie, referral perk, etc.)
10. 4–5 numbers I should track daily to know if this is working (sold vs. 
    prepped, queue time, revenue vs. target, waste %).

GROUND RULES
Every price needs the math behind it, not just a number you picked. Every 
quantity needs the demand logic behind it too. Before finalizing, double-check 
your budget actually sums to ₹10,000 or under, and that your P&L revenue matches 
the prices and quantities you set earlier. Skip vague phrases like "reasonably 
priced" or "a good number" or "during peak hours" — give me the actual number, 
price, or time. And actually apply the theory: thin margins on everyday staples, 
fatter margin on the flagship item, and stagger people rather than just relying 
on price to control the crowd.

Close with your top 3 highest-impact calls in this whole plan, and why.
